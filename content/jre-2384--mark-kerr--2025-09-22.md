They revisit late-90s MMA: elite wrestling as the ultimate base, cardio as the true terror, and the steroid era, with Pride telling fighters “we do not test for steroids,” plus Kerr describing swapping urine and getting paid in room-fulls of cash he once stuffed into cowboy boots to fly home. Kerr recounts nearly facing Royce Gracie before a UFC lawsuit, the Coleman–Maurice Smith turning point on conditioning, and his own hypoglycemic crash against Fujita. They frame modern dominance through wrestlers with endless gears—Velasquez, Merab, Khamzat, Khabib—cite GSP and Jon Jones as peak outliers, and argue some skill gaps are unbridgeable. Rogan slams Foxcatcher’s false fight portrayal, laughs at Kerr’s “chin-in-the-eyeball” submission, and spotlights viral “will-taking” moments like Khabib talking opponents into quitting. They predict the next evolution is a fighter who sprints five hard rounds aided by scientific recovery, while noting money now attracts talent that early pioneers like Kerr and Coleman enabled."
